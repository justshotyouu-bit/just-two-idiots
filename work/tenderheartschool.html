01 The new mark

A tree, a heart and a lamp in one shape.

The old crest was a dense circular seal that fell apart below an inch. The new mark reads the same at gate-sign size and at favicon size: branches forming a heart, a gold flame on a torch, and the school’s Sanskrit motto set beneath it.

A sub-brand for the nursery wing, built from the same mark with a horizontal lockup so it fits a bus panel, a gate board or a letterhead without redrawing anything.
Motto
विद्याधनं सर्वधनं प्रधानं
Set in Devanagari under the wordmark rather than translated away — it is the line the school has used since 1989.

02 The website

A classroom on the homepage, not a stock photo.

The site opens on footage from the school — real children, real classroom, filmed the week we shot the campaign. An admissions ticker runs above and below it, because that is the one thing a visiting parent is looking for.

Indigo bar, gold button
Five links and one Admissions button. The nav is the same on every page and never changes size.
Mark as watermark
The tree sits in the top-right corner of the video at low opacity, which is the only place the logo repeats.
Ticker top and bottom
Admissions Now Open scrolls in gold above and below the fold, in the school’s own type.
Four principles around the mark
Rooted in Values, Pursuit of Excellence, Growth with Purpose, Legacy in Motion — two either side of the logo, so the mark carries the section instead of an illustration.
The numbers, once
34,500 lives impacted, 60 countries, 5,000 students, 350 staff. Stated in a single indigo band and never repeated elsewhere on the site.
1989 beside today
A draggable before/after: the rented classroom the school started in, against the campus that now holds 5,000 students.
Proof, in logos
National and international admissions split by a toggle, running as a slow marquee so the row never looks short.
Two buttons, two entry points — junior and class XI — against the tree drawn as a pale background pattern.
03 House system

Six houses, six banners, one pattern.

Nishtha, Sadbhavna, Veer, Dheer, Gyaan and Srijan each got a colour and a named value. The banner shape is drawn once and recoloured, with the tree pattern embossed into the field — so a new house can be added without new artwork.

Colour carries the meaning
Purple for sincerity, orange for harmony, red for courage. The copy explains the colour rather than the crest.
Printable as flags
The same artwork goes to fabric for assembly and sports day, which is why the shape is a pennant and not a card.
Names in Devanagari roots
Transliterated, not translated, with the English value underneath in small caps.
04 Film & photography

Shot over a normal school week.

No staging, no hired extras. Morning assembly, the mosaic wall, the nursery playground, NCC drill and the basketball squad — the film and the stills come from the same shoot, so the site and the campaign look like one school.

One camera, one week
Assembly to last bell. The library the school now draws on for circulars, admissions creatives and the prospectus.
Uniform as palette
Yellow and navy are already on every child, so the brand colours were chosen to sit with them rather than against them.
Black and white for portraits
Individual students are treated in mono, which keeps a single face from competing with the colour of the campus.
