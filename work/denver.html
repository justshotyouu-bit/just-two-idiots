02 The four fragrances

One bottle per mood, one line per bottle.

Each fragrance got its own colour of glass and its own set. On the product page it is one photo, one name, and two sentences — no note pyramid, no scroll-triggered claims.

Ultra Noir
Matte black glass, a fluted gold collar, the collection's darkest bottle. Bergamot and lemon over warm woody spice — built for after dark.
Regal Musk
Clear glass and a brushed-gold plate, shot in hard daylight yellow. A warm skin musk, the lightest fragrance in the range.
Intense Oud
Deep forest-green glass with a black leather cap. Saffron and rose over agarwood and amber — the collection's richest, longest-wearing pour.
Mystic Ocean
Ribbed cobalt glass, the coolest bottle in the collection. A clean aquatic accord built to survive a humid day.
03 The bottle shoot

Four sets, matched to four scents.

Sandalwood and rope for the oud, a chessboard and warm light for the musk, obsidian and candlelight for the noir, pool water for the ocean. Every set was chosen to match the fragrance, not just the bottle colour.

One prop set per scent
Wood and cinder for the oud and noir, chess and warm daylight for the musk, water for the ocean. The set tells you what the fragrance smells like before you read a word.
Crest and cap, always in frame
Every angle keeps the laurel crest, wordmark and signature legible — the one constant across four very different sets.
Shot for the grid
Same portrait crop and framing across all four, so the collection page reads as one launch rather than four separate products.
04 On the site

Four product pages, one template.

Same layout for every fragrance: hero shot, name, two-line description, then the note breakdown. Buyers can compare all four without relearning a new page each time.
